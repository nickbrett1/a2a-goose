# S18 — Is the first turn on a fresh `contextId` the expensive one?

**Date:** 2026-09-17, 21:41–21:42Z · **Host:** mac-studio · **Release:** `0.1.37` ·
**goose:** 1.50.1 · **Route:** LiteLLM `/a2a/{agent_id}` on the NAS, with the
same 1.0 `SendMessage` envelope a caller sends.

## Question

S17 recorded, in passing, that the first turn on a new `contextId` costs
**~8 minutes** and the next turn on that context **2 s**, and attributed the
difference to *session and extension startup*. That number then went into this
repo's README, S17's verdict and RUNBOOK.md as a latency a caller must budget
for, and into the open tool-call-timeout item (S16 §5). Is it real?

## Method

`/tmp/turn.py` on the host: read `~/.config/a2a-goose/env`, POST a 1.0
`SendMessage` with an explicit `contextId` to `{LITELLM_BASE_URL}/a2a/{agent_id}`,
print wall-clock elapsed and the returned task state. Prompts were chosen to
cost the model nothing ("reply with just the number 3") so the *dispatch* cost
is what is being measured, plus one substantive turn for contrast.

Five runs, at warm and cold payload, singly and at three-way concurrency:

| # | Payload state | Context | Turn | Elapsed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | warm (up ~30 min) | **new** `83351bc7-…` | "reply with just the number 3" | **1.9 s** |
| 2 | `SIGTERM` → registered in **5 s**; first turn after restart | **new** `fff19300-…` | "reply with just the number 11" | **2.4 s** |
| 3 | same | reused `fff19300-…` | "reply with just the number 22" | **1.4 s** |
| 4 | `SIGTERM` → registered in **20 s**; then **3 at once** | three **new** contexts | "reply with just the number 31/32/33" | **2.4 / 2.6 / 2.8 s** |
| 5 | warm | **new** | "use your tools to list my memos and give me the three most recent titles, plus the current UTC time" | **3.9 s** |

Every run: `TASK_STATE_COMPLETED`, one populated `answer` artifact, one empty
second `answer` artifact (the standing item), and the goose CLI log showing
`Restoring evicted session …` with no gap that accounts for minutes.

Run 4 is the one that matters for S17's story: the earlier slow turn happened
while three callers (the hub, another agent, and a `curl`) were all calling a
`goose serve` that had been up only ~3 minutes. Three simultaneous turns on a
just-restarted payload cost **2.4–2.8 s each** — `maxConcurrentSessions: 4`, so
all three ran in parallel, not serialised.

## Verdict

**S17's latency claim is OVERTURNED.** There is no measurable cold-session,
cold-payload or concurrency warm-up on this host: a brand-new `contextId` on a
warm payload is **1.9 s**, the first turn after a `SIGTERM`-restart is **2.4 s**,
a substantive tool-using turn is **3.9 s**.

What the ~8-minute observation actually was, we cannot now reproduce. The two
candidates left are (a) that turn's own work — it was a real request, not a
ping, so its cost was thinking and tool calls, not startup — and (b) provider
latency through LiteLLM at that moment. Nothing in the evidence supports
session or extension startup as the cost, and the README/RUNBOOK advice built
on it ("treat the first turn per context as the expensive one") should not be
acted on.

Consequences:

- A caller's tool-call timeout should be sized to **the task**, not to a
  warm-up. No pre-warm turn at boot is needed, and the idea that pre-warming
  would make the timeout item "a 2-second problem" is moot — it is already 2 s.
- The durable pattern still stands for other reasons: reuse a `contextId` for
  continuity, and have a long-running agent **write its result down** (a memo)
  before explaining, so a caller that gives up at 90 s loses the reply and not
  the work.
- **`agent_id` changes on every restart of the payload** — `cd062df7-…` →
  `556f9b27-…` → `e1de8929-…` across these three restarts. Anything holding a
  literal id goes stale on a host restart; resolve by name (`list_agents`) and
  re-read the id per call.
- `SIGTERM` → `registered` also varies with no load: **8 s** (S17), **5 s**,
  **20 s** here. Budget a minute.

## Reproduce

```sh
# on the host
python3 /tmp/turn.py <agent_id> - "reply with just the number 3" 600   # new context
python3 /tmp/turn.py <agent_id> <that contextId> "reply with just the number 4" 600
# restart and time the first turn
PID=$(ps -Ao pid,args | awk '/current\/bin\/a2a-goose/ && !/awk/ {print $1; exit}')
kill -TERM "$PID"     # never launchctl kickstart -k (S17)
# poll /status until registry.state == registered, then re-run turn.py with a new contextId
```

## What this does not settle

- **Why the hub's `ask_agent` call timed out** (`MCP error -32001`). The turn
  took minutes once; it can presumably take minutes again. That is a caller
  timeout question (S16 §5), and it is unaffected by this spike either way.
- The empty second `answer` artifact, reproducible in all five runs above.
